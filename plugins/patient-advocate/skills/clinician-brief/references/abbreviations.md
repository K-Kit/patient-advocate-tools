# Abbreviation conventions

Concise is the goal, but never at the cost of an abbreviation that could be misread.

## Never use (Joint Commission "Do Not Use" list)

| Don't write | Write instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| U, u | unit | Mistaken for 0, 4, or cc |
| IU | international unit | Mistaken for IV or 10 |
| Q.D., QD, q.d., qd | daily | Mistaken for QID |
| Q.O.D., QOD, q.o.d., qod | every other day | Mistaken for QD or QID |
| Trailing zero (5.0 mg) | 5 mg | Decimal point missed → 10× dose |
| Missing leading zero (.5 mg) | 0.5 mg | Decimal point missed → 10× dose |
| MS, MSO4 | morphine sulfate | Confused with magnesium sulfate |
| MgSO4 | magnesium sulfate | Confused with morphine sulfate |

Also avoid: `µg` (write `mcg`), `cc` (write `mL`), `>` / `<` for doses (write "greater than" / "less than"), `@` (write "at"), and drug-name abbreviations other than widely standard ones.

Exception: lab values copied from the source keep the source's units exactly (e.g. `IU/L` for enzymes is the reported unit — copy it, don't rewrite it).

## Fine to use

Standard clinical shorthand most physicians read without pause:

- **Demographics / history:** y/o, M/F, PMH, PSH, FHx, SHx, s/p, w/, w/o, h/o, c/o, p/w (presents with), ROS, HPI
- **Course:** ED, ICU, HD#, POD#, D/C, f/u, d/t, 2/2 (secondary to), r/o, c/f (concerning for), ddx
- **Meds:** PO, IV, SQ, IM, PRN, BID, TID, QID, q#h, qHS, NKDA, ADR
- **Common conditions:** HTN, HLD, T2DM, CAD, HFrEF/HFpEF, AFib, CKD (with stage), COPD, OSA, GERD, DVT/PE, CVA/TIA, UTI, AKI, ACS, NSTEMI/STEMI
- **Labs / studies:** CBC, BMP, CMP, LFTs, Hgb, Plt, WBC, Cr, eGFR, BUN, K, Na, A1c, TSH, BNP / NT-proBNP, INR, Trop, CXR, CT, MRI, TTE/TEE, ECG, EF
- **Trend notation:** ↑ ↓ → Δ, ×2 (times two), WNL (only if every component is normal)

When unsure whether an abbreviation is universal, spell it out the first time.
